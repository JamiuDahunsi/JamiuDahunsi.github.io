Biography

I am a Researcher in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Carnegie Mellon University, having completed my Master of Science at CMU in Spring 2026. My work investigates the convergence of Infrastructure Security, Artificial Intelligence, and Data Center Energy Systems. I study how intelligent workload orchestration and cyber-physical security frameworks can safeguard critical energy delivery, optimize high-density compute power footprints, and build sustainable computing infrastructure.

At Carnegie Mellon University, I collaborate with Professor Theophilus Benson on modeling connectivity infrastructure dependencies and analyzing resilience dynamics across underrepresented infrastructure environments. Additionally, under the direction of Professor Barry Rawn at the Kigali Collaborative Research Center (KCRC) on a UKRI-funded grant, I co-develop cyber-secure connectivity architectures for Phasor Measurement Units (PMUs) adhering to IEEE C37.118 and OpenPMU ecosystems across utility grid and electric vehicle testbeds.

Complementing my research, I serve as a Graduate Teaching Assistant at CMU for Energy Project Development and Finance and Photovoltaic Systems Engineering, bringing deep domain focus to techno-economic energy modeling and solar power integration. Prior to graduate studies at Carnegie Mellon, I earned my Bachelor of Engineering degree in Telecommunications Engineering from the Federal University of Technology Minna, Nigeria, where my undergraduate thesis investigated cloud-integrated IoT energy telemetry and automated demand-side load curtailment.

Research Interests

Data Center Energy Systems & Power Telemetry

Hyperscale power architectures, microgrid integration, battery energy storage systems (BESS), solar PV integration, high-density power distribution, dynamic thermal modeling, and demand-response load curtailment for large-scale compute facilities.

Critical Infrastructure & OT Cyber Security

Zero-trust architectures for operational technology (OT), cyber-physical power protection, IEEE C37.118 synchronization and OpenPMU ecosystems, substation SCADA security, and defending energy delivery networks against malicious load-manipulation and fault-injection attacks.

AI for Energy & Workload Optimization (AIOps)

Applied machine learning for predictive cooling, carbon-aware cluster workload scheduling, dynamic power management, anomaly detection, transformer-based telemetry forecasting, and autonomous infrastructure orchestration.

Infrastructure Measurement & Systems Resilience

Empirical measurement of network and grid dependencies, large-scale latency and outage telemetry, failure cascade modeling between power and communications systems, and infrastructure equity across expanding compute regions.
